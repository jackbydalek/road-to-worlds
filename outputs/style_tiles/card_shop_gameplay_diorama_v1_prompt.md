# Card Shop Gameplay Diorama v1 — Generation Prompt

Use case: ui-mockup

Asset type: convincing in-game screenshot for the original Godot game “Kitchen Table: Road to Worlds”

Primary request: Create a polished 16:9 gameplay screenshot showing the player exploring the interior of a bright contemporary low-poly card shop. Present the whole room as a charming cutaway diorama viewed from an elevated three-quarter third-person camera, with the front wall removed for clarity, but make it feel like a real playable game screen rather than a concept sheet.

Environment: a modern neighborhood hobby store with pale cream walls, light terrazzo floor, light-oak fixtures, matte teal counters, black track lighting, large glass storefront windows, clean product shelving, colorful booster boxes and deck accessories, acrylic card displays, plants, community posters, and warm daylight. Include a glass singles counter with a friendly shopkeeper, a central trade/deck-tuning bar, a product wall, event registration desk, several open-play tables, and one raised feature table with teal felt.

Player: one original friendly low-poly player avatar standing near the center foreground, viewed mostly from behind, with large head, chunky shoes and hands, polygonal hair, casual jacket and backpack or deck case. Their scale and placement make the room feel explorable. Include several low-poly NPCs browsing, trading, and playing, with clear readable poses and no crowding.

Interaction language: use only subtle game cues—a small warm outline around the currently hovered singles counter, a modest floating button prompt reading “Talk” near the shopkeeper, and very small unobtrusive icons over the trade bar, booster display, event desk, and deck box. Do not label every object.

HUD: minimal contemporary HUD integrated around the edges. Top-left shows “WEEK 3 • SATURDAY” and “$42”. Top-right has small deck, calendar, and settings icons. Bottom-center has one narrow contextual hint: “Choose where to go.” No minimap, quest list, health bar, or large dashboard panels.

Style/medium: visibly low-poly original late-1990s-console-adventure-inspired 3D with modern polish—broad planar geometry, strong silhouettes, selective soft bevels, hand-painted low-resolution textures, subtle vertex gradients, restrained cel shading, slightly pixel-soft texture detail, clean ambient occlusion, readable character faces. Completely original; do not copy any existing franchise, character, costume, interface, or building.

Lighting/mood: bright high-key late-morning daylight through storefront glass, cool sky fill, warm track-light pools, soft contact shadows, cheerful local-community atmosphere, clean exposure with no crushed blacks.

Color palette: parchment cream #F7E9C8, cocoa ink #241B17, light oak and walnut, muted teal #2D6F6A, with red-orange #E94E2F, leafy green #56A044, sky blue #36A3E6, lemon yellow #F4C542 and grape purple #9138A6 on products, posters, table mats, and NPC clothing.

Composition/framing: cutaway diorama fills the screen with shallow depth and strong room readability; player in lower-center foreground; counter to left, product wall to right, trade bar central, play area and feature table deeper in the room; clear pathways between destinations; gentle tilt-shift miniature quality without heavy blur.

Text: “WEEK 3 • SATURDAY”, “$42”, “Talk”, “Choose where to go.”

Constraints: look like a shippable actual game screenshot; modern real card-shop cues; cozy but bright; appealing low poly; clean hierarchy; original branding only. Avoid reference-board panels, annotations, floor-plan labels, photorealism, dark rustic shop, bakery or tavern styling, sci-fi holograms, neon cyberpunk, giant HUD, heavy outlines on every polygon, excessive bloom, copyrighted card products or logos, extra text, and watermark.
