# Kitchen Table 3D World Style Tile v1 — Generation Prompt

Use case: infographic-diagram

Asset type: production 3D-world art-direction style tile for the original Godot card game “Kitchen Table: Road to Worlds”

Primary request: Create one polished landscape 16:9 style tile showing an original late-1990s console-era low-poly 3D adventure world that surrounds a cozy hand-illustrated trading-card game. The world should feel chunky, colorful, optimistic, expressive, highly readable, and appealing rather than like an unfinished greybox.

Scene/backdrop: clean warm presentation board with a modular editorial grid. The hero module is a small neighborhood card-shop street: squat teal-and-cream shop, oversized awning and hanging card sign, simplified rooftop vents, planter boxes, chunky sidewalk, low-poly trees, colorful tournament posters, and an inviting orange door. Include a second small interior vignette showing a warm card counter, simple shelves, booster boxes, and a felt tabletop.

Style/medium: visibly low-poly 3D render; broad planar geometry, simplified silhouettes, selective rounded bevels, hand-painted low-resolution textures, subtle vertex-color gradients, restrained cel shading, painted ambient occlusion, graphic face details, slightly pixel-soft texture edges. Evoke the spirit of an optimistic late-90s action-adventure console game while remaining completely original and not copying any existing franchise, character, costume, building, logo, or UI.

Composition/framing: clearly separated modules for (1) hero exterior environment render, (2) character proportion lineup with player, shopkeeper, and tournament clerk silhouettes, (3) architecture kit with door, awning, window, roof vent, curb and sign, (4) material and texture swatches, (5) lighting recipe with warm sun and cool sky fill, (6) prop kit including card display, deck box, booster crate, poster, stool and vending machine, (7) a close-up showing a cozy detailed paper card held inside the simplified 3D world, and (8) silhouette and bevel rules. Strong hierarchy, generous spacing, production-ready visual reference.

Character direction: stylized humans with large heads, expressive graphic eyes, chunky hands and shoes, simplified cylindrical limbs, readable poses, compact five-to-six-head-tall proportions, polygonal hair clumps, flat painted clothing, friendly silhouettes. The shopkeeper has an apron but is fully original.

Color palette: preserve the established warm card-game system—parchment cream #F7E9C8, cocoa ink #241B17, walnut #57372E and muted teal #2D6F6A—with Spicy red-orange #E94E2F, Hearty leafy green #56A044, Sweet sky blue #36A3E6, Fresh lemon yellow #F4C542 and Funky grape purple #9138A6 used as signage, clothing and prop accents. Clear blue-green sky and warm honey lighting.

Materials/textures: painted plaster, chunky wood, matte colorful plastic, faded metal, felt playmats, low-poly foliage, paper posters and illustrated card stock. Minimal realistic reflectivity; no glossy PBR showroom look.

Rendering rules: one warm key light, cool ambient sky fill, restrained contact shadows, minimal bloom, no photorealism, no noisy microdetail, no extreme texture warping, no harsh black outlines around every polygon. Cards may be higher-detail and tactile so they visibly pop against the simpler world.

Text (verbatim, use only these short labels): “3D WORLD”, “SHAPE LANGUAGE”, “CHARACTERS”, “ARCHITECTURE KIT”, “MATERIALS”, “LIGHTING”, “PROP KIT”, “COZY CARDS / CHUNKY WORLD”.

Constraints: cohesive with a handmade food-creature card game; visually useful to a 3D artist and Godot developer; show specific practical examples; charming and adventurous; custom original designs only; no copyrighted characters, logos, card packs, or franchise-specific motifs; no watermark; no dense paragraphs; no black dashboard UI; no realistic humans; no futuristic sci-fi city; no grim dystopia.
